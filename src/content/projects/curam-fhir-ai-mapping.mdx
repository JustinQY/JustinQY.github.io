## At a glance

From January to March 2026, I independently designed and built a **multi-agent mapping assessment framework** for IBM Curam → HL7 FHIR R4. Specialized Copilot roles divide planning, mapping, review, and orchestration; reusable Skills guide domain reasoning, while Python checks and BA feedback evaluate the results.

<CuramMappingOverview links={frontmatter.links} />

## The challenge

Curam social-services tables and FHIR resources do not map one-to-one. A broad prompt to “map these tables and summarize the results” combines business interpretation, resource selection, generation, and checking in one response. I decomposed those responsibilities into **bounded tasks, explicit rules, and review gates**, so proposals could be inspected before processing more tables.

## My contribution

I defined four cooperating agent roles and four reusable Skills, implemented the validator and progress tracking, and built the Python preparation and Excel export tools. **Technical design and implementation were entirely my responsibility; BAs assessed business usefulness.**

<CuramMappingDesign kind="agents" />

## How the workflow works

<CuramMappingWorkflow />

The preparation script downloads and normalizes table metadata, checks counts, and generates Word batches—35 tables by default. After planner initialization, **one orchestrator prompt drives all remaining batches**, invoking mapper and reviewer as subagents around the Python validation gate. Progress distinguishes mapped from reviewed batches; failures pause the run for correction. Excel export is started separately.

## Skills that guide the reasoning

Four task-specific `SKILL.md` files turn domain knowledge into reusable guidance. They define permissible choices, evidence to consider, and mistakes to challenge, aiming to reduce unsupported conclusions and irrelevant output.

<CuramMappingDesign kind="skills" />

Within this assessment, FHIR `Task` is reserved for caseworker work items, not workflow-engine runtime records. Each proposal records source context, candidate decisions, precedent use, native mappings, and extension needs. Required extensions force `PARTIAL_MATCH`, making gaps visible to reviewers.

I separated **rule validity, semantic fit, and business usefulness** into three evaluation layers:

The orchestrator advances only after validation and reviewer approval. These checks support a reviewable assessment; the Python script does not yet validate every resource name against the full R4 list or verify every target element path.

## Results and business value

Across the project, I processed **2,000+ tables**. BA review of the **full set** found **over 95% of mappings effective**, and the framework **avoided the need to hire two FHIR engineers** for this work. The effectiveness figure measures BA-assessed usefulness, not independently verified FHIR conformance.

The final Excel summaries below cover **CPIN** and **DW**. Their full/partial/no-match distributions describe fit to FHIR; the high share needing extensions shows why explicit gap reporting mattered.

<CuramMappingResults />

## What I learned

The central design choice was making **generation, evidence, and evaluation separate responsibilities**. Next steps are a formal BA acceptance rubric, complete R4 resource/path checks, and a measured single-prompt baseline to quantify error reduction and review effort.
